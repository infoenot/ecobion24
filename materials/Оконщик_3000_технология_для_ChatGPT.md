## 1. What the product is (one paragraph)

We make **one-piece bent profiles** (boxes, covers, reveals, sills, frames) from **10 mm PVC sandwich panel** on a small workshop machine called **"Okonshchik 3000"**. The main use: covering the **cold aluminium mullions and transoms of floor-to-ceiling glazing** in Russian new-build apartments ("thermal boxes"). The aluminium profile is first insulated, then the box is glued on top. The result looks like a slim, clean, seamless painted or wood-grain casing over each mullion. The same technology makes window and door reveals, sills, skirting, column cladding, beams, frames for mirrors and maps, and similar straight-line profiles.

## 2. The material

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | PVC sandwich panel, 10 mm |
| Structure | rigid PVC skin 0.7–1 mm + extruded polystyrene foam (XPS) core + rigid PVC skin 0.7–1 mm |
| Total thickness | 10 mm |
| Sheet size | 1500 × 3000 mm or 2000 × 3000 mm |
| Base colour | matte white, like a white PVC window |
| Finishes | painted in any RAL colour (matte); or laminated with PVC film: wood grain (light oak, golden oak, Irish oak, walnut, ash), plain colours, black matte, kids' patterns |
| Surface | smooth, matte or satin, uniform; laminate film has realistic fine grain, no gloss |
| Weight | light; boxes are thin-walled, not solid |

Visual cue: at a cut end you would see a thin white/coloured skin, a pale foam core, and another skin — like a 10 mm "sandwich". At finished installations the ends are hidden or closed.

## 3. How the machine makes a profile

The machine is a 3.2 m long table (600 × 900 × 3200 mm) with a guide rail, a cutter carriage, a grooving carriage and a heated wire.

1. **Cut a strip** (developed width) from the sheet along the rail.
2. **Cut a groove** on the inside along each future bend line.
3. **Heat** the bend line with a hot nichrome wire.
4. **Fold** the strip to **exactly 90°**.

Result: a **single piece** with **straight, sharp 90° folds** and a **continuous outer edge** — no joint, no glue line, no seam on the visible corner. Outer corner radius is tiny (≈1 mm).

What the machine can make:
- L-profile (1 bend), U-profile (2 bends), Z/step profile, W-profile (double U), closed square/rectangle (4 bends, one hidden joint), sill with drip edge (3 bends).
- Length up to 3 m in one piece (sheet length). Longer runs are joined end-to-end with a neat butt joint.
- Faceted shapes (octagon for round columns, polygon for bay windows) — from several flat pieces.

What it **cannot** make:
- No curves, no arcs, no rounded surfaces, no bent-round boxes.
- No angles other than 90° in one fold (polygons are assembled from separate flat faces).
- Not windows, not glass, not aluminium profiles.

## 4. How a mullion box is installed (the "layer cake")

On a cold aluminium mullion of floor-to-ceiling glazing:

1. **Waterproofing:** the metal is wrapped with butyl tape or coated with liquid rubber (black/grey layer).
2. **Insulation:** extruded polystyrene boards (often pink/orange or blue XPS) are glued on the mullion faces.
3. **Filling and sealing:** gaps filled with foam; joints sealed; a rubber strip between glass unit and box.
4. **Trial fit:** a test box is tried on, final size measured to the millimetre.
5. **Fitting:** the final box is slid over and **glued** (sealant/foam adhesive). **No screws, no drilling** into the aluminium.

So in "during installation" images: dark aluminium, black butyl tape, coloured XPS foam on the post, a white box being slid on. In "finished" images: only the clean box is visible.

## 5. What a finished installation looks like

- Each vertical mullion: one slim U-box **floor to ceiling**, typically **100–160 mm wide**, 60–120 mm deep, flush and straight.
- Horizontal transoms: L- or U-boxes, meeting vertical boxes with **neat 90° butt joints**.
- Glass and sightlines stay the same: the box covers only the metal, the window opening is not reduced.
- Joints to wall, floor, ceiling: thin, neat sealant line in matching colour.
- Typical look: white boxes on dark aluminium glazing (big contrast before/after); or boxes in the room's palette (graphite, black, wood).
- Often combined with a **deep window sill** on the low transom (golden oak, white) and **3D reveals** on window/door openings.

## 6. Typical settings (where it is used)

- Glazed loggias and balconies in Moscow-region new-build towers (business class), floor-to-ceiling aluminium glazing with thin dark mullions (grey, black, brown, sometimes green/teal).
- Living rooms and bedrooms with panoramic "French" windows and bay windows.
- Kitchens where a balcony door block was removed — the low transom becomes a counter/sill.
- Country houses: terraces with steel posts, winter gardens, indoor pools.
- Offices, cafes, shopping malls: glass partitions, columns, storefront parapets.

## 7. Why the customer pays (for scene mood)

- **Warm:** in winter bare aluminium mullions cool to +10…+16 °C, condensation and frost appear. Boxed = no cold metal visible.
- **Quiet:** hollow mullions carry sound between floors; filled and boxed posts carry less.
- **Dry:** waterproofed metal, no condensation on finishes.
- **Beautiful:** developer's dark, scratched profiles are replaced by clean boxes in any colour; the window fits the interior design.
- **Usable space:** a cold storage loggia becomes an office, kids' corner or plant room (together with floor and ceiling insulation).

## 9. Hard rules for accurate images

DO:
- Slim, straight, flush profiles; crisp 90° folds; continuous outer edges.
- Matte or satin finishes; realistic wood grain with fine variation.
- Proportions true to life: mullion box 100–160 mm wide on a 2.7–3 m high glazing.
- Same glass size before and after.

DON'T:
- No curved or rounded boxes, no bulky casings, no thick trims.
- No LED strips or lighting inside/along the boxes.
- No visible screws, nails, brackets.
- No glossy plastic look, no "toy" look.
- No glazing replaced by walls, no reduced windows.
- No impossible joints (mitred curves, floating parts).
- No text, logos, brand names.
